From historical wildfire exposure to near-real-time threat monitoring

A proposed satellite-based service to help organisations understand wildfire exposure and monitor emerging threats to critical infrastructure and distributed assets.

Discuss a pilot application Read about the services

The opportunity

With climate change lengthening fire seasons and extending wildfire activity into new regions, wildfire hazards pose growing challenges for energy, transport, telecommunications and industrial infrastructure. Asset managers need information that connects long-term exposure with the evolution of ongoing fire events.

IGNIS SPECULA aims to bring these perspectives together through Earth observation, environmental information and advanced analysis.

Two complementary services

One view of the hazard, from the historical record to the fire happening now.

ILong-term wildfire risk assessment

Time scale: years

Retrospective analysis of wildfire activity and environmental conditions to characterise historical exposure, identify spatial and seasonal patterns, and compare risks across sites or asset portfolios.

Potential uses

  • Infrastructure planning
  • Prioritisation of mitigation measures
  • Risk or insurance assessments

IINear-real-time wildfire monitoring

Time scale: days to half-hours

Frequent observation of active fires to support awareness of events developing near assets of interest. The service would aim to provide timely information on fire location, evolution and potential exposure.

Helping users

  • Track fire location and evolution
  • Prioritise attention across assets
  • Inform operational decisions

Potential users and applications

The services are intended for organisations managing assets spread across fire-prone landscapes:

  • Wind and solar farms
  • Electricity networks
  • Transport corridors
  • Telecommunications infrastructure
  • Industrial sites

They may also be relevant to insurers, risk-management organisations and civil protection agencies.

Development and partnership opportunity

The concept builds on research expertise in wildfire science, satellite observations and environmental modelling. An upcoming European Space Agency (ESA) funding opportunity could support the development and demonstration of an operational service with an industrial partner.

At this stage, we are seeking discussions with potential end users to better understand their needs and identify relevant pilot applications.

Example: wildfire exposure across South Africa, 2003–2025

From 23 years of satellite fire observations, we reconstructed about 135,000 fire events across South Africa. The map shows, for each 5 km² cell, how often fire returns and how intensely it burns. Dark blue marks areas that burn often and intensely, such as the eastern grasslands of Mpumalanga and KwaZulu-Natal. Pink marks areas that burn rarely but intensely, such as the fynbos mountains of the Western Cape: rare does not mean safe. Overall, 38 % of the country was hit at least once.

The two charts turn this history into annual exceedance figures that risk and asset managers can use directly. Each year about 1,000 fires exceed 1,000 ha, about one exceeds 25,000 ha, and around ten have a p80 spread rate above 0.4 km/h. Applied to a site, a power line or a whole portfolio, the same method gives local fire frequency, intensity and likely spread rates to support siting, mitigation and insurance decisions.
